I am an independent researcher in Yerevan, Armenia, where I moved from Russia in 2022 after the start of the war in Ukraine. I study how transposable elements (TEs) and the human genome have shaped each other, and how the conflict between them shows up again in cancer. Since April 2026 I have led a project on follicular lymphoma in the [Group of Bioinformatics]({{ site.data.affiliation.group_url }}), headed by Arsen Arakelyan, at the Institute of Molecular Biology of the National Academy of Sciences of the Republic of Armenia. In parallel, I study TE evolution using public data: ENCODE, GTEx and the telomere-to-telomere T2T-CHM13 assembly. "Daniil Nikitin's lab" is this research line, and I am its only member.

## Research questions

- Which human genes and molecular processes acquired regulatory elements from transposable elements, and how recently?
- How do young TE subfamilies escape host silencing by H3K9me3 and H3K27me3, and what do they gain when they do?
- Can transcriptomes from different platforms be harmonized well enough to separate closely related lymphomas, such as follicular lymphoma and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma?

## Recent work

In 2026 I published two single-author studies on TE–host genome evolution. In [*G3: Genes, Genomes, Genetics*](https://doi.org/10.1093/g3journal/jkag250), I mapped 3,709,429 TEs to 28,738 human genes on the T2T-CHM13 assembly and found a 220 kb interferon-alpha domain in which 77 of 175 TEs are young L1 copies. In [*Royal Society Open Science*](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.260639), I analysed seven epigenomic modalities in twelve cell lines and showed that SVA elements carry the strongest signatures of escape from H3K9me3-mediated heterochromatinization.

The follicular lymphoma project produced its first preprint in September 2026: [ComboBatch](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.751825), a benchmark of 2,234 transcriptomic harmonization approaches on 7,174 samples from 88 germinal-center B-cell lymphoma cohorts.

## Background

I graduated from the Department of Molecular Biology, Faculty of Biology, Lomonosov Moscow State University (2013–2019), and studied in the PhD program of the Department of Genomics and Postgenome Technologies at the Shemyakin–Ovchinnikov Institute of Bioorganic Chemistry, Russian Academy of Sciences (2019–2022). From 2017 to 2021 I worked with Anton Buzdin's group on the regulatory evolution of human retroelements, which resulted in five articles based on ENCODE ChIP-seq and GTEx data.

I also recorded a 35-lecture course on molecular biology in Russian. Eight lectures that follow an evolutionary line are collected on the [video course page](/course/).
